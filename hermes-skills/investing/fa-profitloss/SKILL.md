# FA Profit & Loss Analysis — Leaf Skill

Analyze the income statement (P&L) of an NSE-listed company using a 10-point checklist. For each criterion: state the finding with actual numbers, cite the data source, and assign a RAG verdict.

## Input Required

Before starting, confirm availability of the P&L data packet from:
- fa-profitloss-agent: Revenue trends, expense breakdown, EBITDA/EBIT/PBT/PAT, margins, growth rates, segment revenue, consolidated vs standalone comparison

If agent data is missing, note "Data unavailable — unable to verify" for affected criteria rather than assuming clean.

## Mindset

> "The company you work for provides you with a salary slip. Does providing a salary slip alone suffice, or do they need to deposit money in your account?" — Money Purse
>
> The P&L statement is like the salary slip — it shows what the company *claims* to have earned. Revenue and profit numbers reported in the P&L are NOT necessarily cash. Companies book sales before cash is received (credit periods). Some companies even inflate numbers to boost share price. Always cross-check with cash flow analysis (fa-cashflow skill).

## Why P&L Analysis Matters

1. **Revenue quality** — Is revenue growing consistently from core operations, or padded with one-time items?
2. **Margin sustainability** — Are operating margins stable or being squeezed by rising costs?
3. **Expense discipline** — Is management keeping costs under control as the business scales?
4. **Earnings manipulation detection** — Exceptional items, volatile tax rates, and widening gap between standalone and consolidated numbers can signal problems
5. **Growth trajectory** — Revenue and PAT growth rates reveal whether the business is compounding or stagnating

## Important Caveats (apply before analysis)

1. **P&L is a period statement** — Numbers represent a specific period (quarter, half-year, full year), not a snapshot like balance sheet.
2. **PAT is NOT cash** — Companies provide credit periods to customers (30-60+ days). Sales are booked before cash arrives. Always verify with cash flow statement.
3. **Consolidated vs Standalone matters** — Use consolidated for companies with significant unlisted subsidiaries. Use standalone for companies whose listed subsidiaries perform independently (e.g., HDFC). Check both if there is a large gap.
4. **Banks, NBFCs & HFCs have different P&L structure** — NII (Net Interest Income) replaces Revenue from Operations, Provisions replace COGS. Flag this and adjust analysis accordingly.
5. **One bad year is not conclusive** — Check multi-year trends. COVID-impacted FY20-21 numbers should be contextualized.

## The 10-Point P&L Checklist

Work through each criterion in order. For each criterion:
1. State the specific numbers found
2. Apply the threshold rules below
3. Assign RAG status
4. Note the source (Screener.in URL, AR page number, etc.)

### Criterion 1: Revenue Growth Consistency

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (Revenue from Operations trend, growth rates)
**Check:** Is revenue from operations growing consistently? Erratic or declining revenue signals business trouble.

**Thresholds:**
- Revenue CAGR (5yr) > 15% AND positive in 4+ of 5 years -> Green (strong, consistent growth)
- Revenue CAGR (5yr) 8-15% AND positive in 3+ of 5 years -> Amber (moderate growth)
- Revenue CAGR (5yr) < 8% OR negative in 3+ of 5 years -> Red (stagnating or declining)
- Revenue declined for 2+ consecutive years -> bump up one level

**Sector adjustments:**
- Mature/stable sectors (FMCG, Utilities): 8-12% CAGR is strong -> adjust thresholds by -5%
- High-growth sectors (IT, Pharma, Consumer Tech): Expect higher CAGR -> apply stricter thresholds

**Report format:**
```
Revenue from Operations (last 5 years):
FY__: Rs.[X] cr | FY__: Rs.[X] cr | FY__: Rs.[X] cr | FY__: Rs.[X] cr | FY__: Rs.[X] cr
YoY Growth: [X%] | [X%] | [X%] | [X%]
Revenue CAGR (3yr): [X%]
Revenue CAGR (5yr): [X%]
```

### Criterion 2: EBITDA Margin Stability & Level

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (EBITDA and EBITDA Margin trends)
**Check:** Is the EBITDA margin stable or improving? Declining margins indicate cost pressures or pricing weakness. EBITDA = profit generated by the core business before financial and accounting adjustments.

**Thresholds:**
- EBITDA Margin > 20% AND stable/improving over 5 years -> Green (strong operating efficiency)
- EBITDA Margin 12-20% AND stable -> Amber (adequate but monitor)
- EBITDA Margin < 12% OR declining consistently over 3+ years -> Red (thin or deteriorating margins)
- EBITDA Margin swing > 10 percentage points in a single year -> Amber (investigate cause)

**Sector adjustments:**
- IT/Software: EBITDA margins of 25-35% are normal -> apply higher thresholds (+10%)
- Trading/Commodity: EBITDA margins of 5-10% are normal -> adjust thresholds by -8%
- FMCG: 15-25% is healthy range
- Capital goods/EPC: 10-15% is typical

**Report format:**
```
EBITDA Margin (last 5 years):
FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%]
Average (5yr): [X%]
Trend: [Improving / Stable / Deteriorating]
```

### Criterion 3: PAT Margin & Net Profitability

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (PAT and PAT Margin trends)
**Check:** Is the company converting revenue into bottom-line profit efficiently? PAT margin shows what percentage of revenue the shareholders actually get.

**Thresholds:**
- PAT Margin > 10% AND stable/improving -> Green
- PAT Margin 5-10% AND stable -> Amber (adequate but thin)
- PAT Margin < 5% OR declining consistently -> Red (razor-thin profitability)
- PAT negative in any year -> Red for that year; if 2+ years in last 5 -> Red overall
- PAT growing faster than revenue (margin expansion) -> positive signal (note even if already Green)

**Sector adjustments:**
- IT/Software: PAT margins of 15-25% are normal -> higher thresholds
- Banking: PAT margin interpreted differently — use ROA/ROE instead if available
- Trading: 2-5% may be normal -> adjust thresholds by -5%

**Report format:**
```
PAT Margin (last 5 years):
FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%]
Average (5yr): [X%]
PAT CAGR (5yr): [X%]
```

### Criterion 4: Expense Ratio Discipline

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (Expense Ratios as % of Revenue)
**Check:** Are major expense heads (raw material, employees, other expenses) as a percentage of revenue stable? Rising expense ratios eat into margins.

**Items to evaluate:**
1. **Raw Material Cost %** — Rising = input cost pressure or loss of pricing power
2. **Employee Cost %** — Gradual rise is okay for talent-intensive businesses; sharp spike is a concern
3. **Other Expenses %** — Should be stable or declining with scale

**Thresholds:**
- All major expense ratios stable or declining over 5 years -> Green (cost discipline)
- One expense ratio rising while others stable -> Amber (investigate which and why)
- Two or more expense ratios rising simultaneously -> Red (margin compression)
- Total expense ratio (Total Expenses / Revenue) declining -> positive signal

**Report format:**
```
Expense Ratios as % of Revenue (last 5 years):
                   FY__   FY__   FY__   FY__   FY__
Raw Material:      [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]
Employee Cost:     [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]
Other Expenses:    [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]
Total Exp Ratio:   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]   [X%]
```

### Criterion 5: Interest Coverage & Finance Cost Burden

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (Finance Costs trend, EBITDA/EBIT trend)
**Calculation:** Interest Coverage Ratio = EBITDA / Finance Costs (preferred, Money Purse P10)

**Why EBITDA not EBIT?** (Money Purse P10) — Depreciation & Amortization are non-cash expenses recorded only in books. If the company can't pay interest, the cash set aside for D&A can be used. So EBITDA better reflects true debt-servicing capacity. Conservative approach: use EBIT.

**Thresholds:** (Money Purse P10: > 3 healthy, < 2 avoid)
- Interest Coverage > 5x -> Green (very comfortable debt servicing)
- Interest Coverage 3-5x -> Green (healthy per Money Purse P10)
- Interest Coverage 2-3x -> Amber (adequate but watch)
- Interest Coverage < 2x -> Red (debt servicing strain — "better to avoid" per Money Purse P10)
- Interest Coverage < 1x -> Red (EBITDA insufficient to cover interest — company may default. Jet Airways, Videocon, DHFL examples)
- Zero debt company (no finance costs) -> Green (note as "Debt-free")

**Additional check — Finance Cost burden:** Is finance cost as % of revenue rising?
- Finance Cost < 3% of revenue AND stable -> Green
- Finance Cost 3-8% of revenue -> Amber
- Finance Cost > 8% of revenue -> Red

**Supplementary check — Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR):** (Money Purse P10)
- Formula: EBITDA / Total Debt Service (Interest + Principal repayment)
- DSCR > 2 → comfortable (can pay interest + principal easily)
- DSCR 1.5-2 → healthy
- DSCR 1-1.5 → tight — monitor closely
- DSCR < 1 → company cannot service its debt — 🔴 avoid completely
- Note: Principal repayment data may not be readily available from Screener.in. If unavailable, skip DSCR and rely on ICR.

**Cross-check note** (Money Purse P10): Screener.in ICR numbers may occasionally be incorrect. Cross-check with Tijori Finance if the number seems off.

**Report format:**
```
Interest Coverage Ratio (last 5 years, using EBITDA):
FY__: EBITDA Rs.[X] cr / Interest Rs.[X] cr = [X]x
FY__: [X]x | FY__: [X]x | FY__: [X]x | FY__: [X]x
Finance Cost as % of Revenue: [X%] (latest FY)
DSCR (if data available): [X]x
```

### Criterion 6: Exceptional Items & One-Time Charges

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (Exceptional Items)
**Check:** Frequent exceptional items distort true earnings. One-time items are normal occasionally but persistent "exceptional" items suggest earnings management.

**Thresholds:**
- No exceptional items in last 5 years -> Green (clean earnings)
- Exceptional items in 1-2 of 5 years with clear explanation -> Amber (monitor)
- Exceptional items in 3+ of 5 years -> Red (pattern of one-off adjustments — not really one-off)
- Single exceptional item > 25% of PBT -> Amber (significant impact — check nature)
- Exceptional gains inflating profit -> Amber (profit quality concern — not from operations)

**Report format:**
```
Exceptional Items (last 5 years):
FY__: Rs.[X] cr — [Nature]
FY__: Rs.[X] cr — [Nature]
...
Years with exceptional items: [X] of 5
```

### Criterion 7: Revenue Quality — Other Income Dependency

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (Other Income trend, Revenue from Operations)
**Check:** Is a large portion of total income coming from "Other Income" (interest, dividends, asset sales) rather than core operations? High other income dependency signals weak operating business.

**Thresholds:**
- Other Income < 5% of Total Revenue -> Green (core business driven)
- Other Income 5-15% of Total Revenue -> Amber (some dependency — check if recurring interest income)
- Other Income > 15% of Total Revenue -> Red (significant non-operating contribution)
- Other Income growing faster than operating revenue -> Amber (over-reliance increasing)

**Exception:** Investment companies (holdcos) naturally have high other income — adjust accordingly.

**Report format:**
```
Other Income as % of Total Revenue (last 5 years):
FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%]
Nature of Other Income (latest FY): [Interest / Dividend / Asset Sale / Other]
```

### Criterion 8: Tax Rate Consistency

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (Tax Expense and PBT trends)
**Calculation:** Effective Tax Rate = Tax Expense / PBT x 100

**Check:** A healthy company pays a consistent, reasonable tax rate (typically 22-30% for Indian companies post-2019 reforms). Volatile or unusually low tax rates may indicate tax havens, aggressive tax planning, or deferred tax adjustments.

**Thresholds:**
- Effective Tax Rate 20-30% AND stable -> Green (normal, consistent tax payer)
- Effective Tax Rate varies by >8 percentage points year-to-year -> Amber (investigate)
- Effective Tax Rate consistently < 15% -> Amber (check for SEZ benefits, tax holidays — may be legitimate)
- Effective Tax Rate > 35% -> Amber (check for deferred tax liability adjustments)
- Negative tax (tax credit) in a profitable year -> Amber (unusual — check MAT credit, deferred tax)

**Report format:**
```
Effective Tax Rate (last 5 years):
FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%] | FY__: [X%]
Average (5yr): [X%]
Consistency: [Stable / Volatile]
```

---

### Criterion 9: Consolidated vs Standalone Gap

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (Consolidated vs Standalone Comparison)
**Check:** A large gap between consolidated and standalone numbers reveals subsidiary performance. If consolidated PAT is much lower than standalone, subsidiaries are loss-making. If much higher, subsidiaries are driving growth but standalone business may be weakening.

**Thresholds:**
- Gap < 10% of standalone revenue AND subsidiaries profitable -> Green (minimal subsidiary impact)
- Gap 10-30% OR subsidiary PAT negative -> Amber (significant subsidiary impact — investigate)
- Gap > 30% AND consolidated PAT significantly lower than standalone -> Red (subsidiaries destroying value)
- Company has no subsidiaries -> N/A

**Additional checks:**
- If consolidated revenue >> standalone but consolidated PAT << standalone -> subsidiaries are revenue-rich but loss-making -> Red
- If management has addressed loss-making subsidiaries in AR (e.g., plans to divest) -> reduce severity by one level

**Report format:**
```
Consolidated vs Standalone (latest FY):
  Revenue: Consol Rs.[X] cr vs Standalone Rs.[X] cr (gap: [X%])
  PAT: Consol Rs.[X] cr vs Standalone Rs.[X] cr (gap: [X%])
  Subsidiary contribution: [Positive / Negative / Neutral]
```

### Criterion 10: EPS Growth & Earnings Quality

**Data source:** fa-profitloss-agent (EPS trend, PAT trend, Dividend Payout)
**Check:** Is EPS (Earnings Per Share) growing consistently? EPS is the ultimate per-share profitability measure for shareholders.

**Thresholds:**
- EPS CAGR (5yr) > 15% AND positive in 4+ of 5 years -> Green (strong earnings compounder)
- EPS CAGR (5yr) 8-15% -> Amber (moderate growth)
- EPS CAGR (5yr) < 8% OR EPS declining -> Red
- EPS negative in any year -> Red for that year; if 2+ years in last 5 -> Red overall
- Dilution check: If shares outstanding increased significantly (>10% in 5 years) AND EPS growth is lower than PAT growth -> Amber (dilutive equity raises)

**Additional signal:** Dividend payout
- Consistent dividend with payout ratio 20-40% -> positive signal (discipline)
- Payout > 80% -> Amber (not reinvesting enough for growth)
- No dividend despite profits -> Amber only if company is not in growth phase

**Report format:**
```
EPS (last 5 years):
FY__: Rs.[X] | FY__: Rs.[X] | FY__: Rs.[X] | FY__: Rs.[X] | FY__: Rs.[X]
EPS CAGR (5yr): [X%]
Dividend Payout (latest FY): [X%]
```

## Aggregating the P&L Score

After completing all 10 criteria:

1. Count: Greens, Ambers, Reds (exclude N/A from count)
2. Apply override rules:
   - PAT negative for 3+ of last 5 years -> **P&L = Red regardless**
   - EBITDA Margin declining for 4+ consecutive years -> **P&L = Red regardless**
   - Revenue declining for 3+ consecutive years -> **P&L = Red regardless**
3. Otherwise:
   - 0 Red, <= 2 Amber -> Green (P&L Strong)
   - 1 Red OR 3 Amber -> Amber (P&L Adequate with Concerns)
   - 2+ Red OR 4+ Amber -> Red (P&L Weak)

## Data Sources (in priority order)

1. **Screener.in** — Primary source for 5-10 year P&L data, margins, ratios
2. **BSE India** — Annual Reports for segment revenue, exceptional items detail, management commentary
3. **Moneycontrol** — Fallback for P&L data if Screener.in unavailable
4. **Tickertape.in** — Secondary fallback, useful for margin charts
5. **Company Investor Relations page** — Annual Reports, investor presentations, quarterly earnings calls

Always use live data from these sources. Never use memorized or cached financial figures.
