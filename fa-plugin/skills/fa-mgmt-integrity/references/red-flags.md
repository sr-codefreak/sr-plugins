# Management Integrity Red Flags — Reference Guide

## SEBI Enforcement Actions

### Types of SEBI Actions (Severity Order)


| Action Type             | Severity    | What It Means                                                     |
| ----------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Debarment from markets  | Critical 🔴 | Promoter banned from accessing capital markets for X years        |
| Disgorgement order      | High 🔴     | SEBI ordered return of illegally gained profits                   |
| Penalty order           | High 🔴     | Monetary penalty for proven violation                             |
| Show Cause Notice (SCN) | Medium 🟡   | Preliminary inquiry stage; not conclusive                         |
| Settlement order        | Medium 🟡   | Resolved via payment without admission of guilt; still a red flag |
| Administrative warning  | Low 🟡      | Minor procedural violation; note but not critical                 |


### How to Search SEBI Orders

1. Go to sebi.gov.in → Enforcement → Orders
2. Search by: (a) Company name, (b) Promoter/Director name
3. Also search SEBI's adjudication orders database
4. Check the NSE/BSE company disclosure page for "Other Disclosures" → SEBI notices

### SFIO (Serious Fraud Investigation Office)

- Under Ministry of Corporate Affairs
- Investigates serious financial fraud in companies
- Any SFIO investigation = 🔴 hard red flag
- Search: mca.gov.in or news search "[Company] SFIO"

### ED (Enforcement Directorate)

- Investigates money laundering (PMLA) and foreign exchange violations (FEMA)
- Any ED proceedings = 🔴 hard red flag
- Often follows SFIO or SEBI action

## Sujata Dalal and Moneylife — India's Fraud Watchlist

### Who is Sujata Dalal?

Sujata Dalal is a financial investigative journalist and co-founder of Moneylife Foundation. Over 20+ years, she has exposed numerous corporate frauds, governance failures, and investor-unfriendly practices at Indian listed companies before they became mainstream news. Her coverage of:

- Satyam Computer (pre-scandal warning signs)
- DHFL (multiple governance flags before collapse)
- IL&FS (early warning on NPAs)
- Multiple smaller companies with related-party abuse

Her articles at moneylife.in are considered one of the best secondary sources for corporate governance red flags in Indian equities.

### How to Check

1. Search: `site:moneylife.in "[Company Name]"` — look for negative coverage
2. Search: `site:moneylife.in "[Promoter Name]"` — direct promoter mentions
3. Also search Moneylife Foundation investor complaints database: `moneylifefoundation.com "[company]"`

### Interpreting Results

- No Moneylife coverage at all → ⬜ (neutral; small company may just not be covered)
- Factual reporting of company's business → ⬜ (neutral)
- Specific governance concern or fraud allegation article → 🔴 (investigate further)
- Multiple articles over time documenting pattern → 🔴 (serious)

**Important:** A Moneylife flag does not mean the company is fraudulent — it means a credible investigative journalist found something worth flagging. Always cross-verify with primary sources (Annual Reports, SEBI orders, BSE filings).

## Related Party Transaction Manipulation Patterns

These patterns are warning signs of promoters extracting value via RPT:

### Pattern 1: Above-Market Purchase Prices

Company buys goods/services from a promoter entity at prices 20-30% above market. The promoter entity earns a margin that should have accrued to the listed company's P&L.

### Pattern 2: Below-Market Sales Prices

Company sells products to a promoter entity at below-market prices. The promoter entity then sells at market price, capturing the margin outside the listed company.

### Pattern 3: Promoter Loans

Listed company gives loans/advances to promoter entities at below-market interest rates or with minimal security. Often classified as "advances" to avoid RPT scrutiny.

### Pattern 4: Asset Rentals

Company rents office space, plant, or equipment from promoter-owned entities at above-market rates.

### Pattern 5: Brand/Trademark Royalties

Company pays royalties to a promoter holding company for use of the brand name. Royalty % often increases over time without justification.

### How to Detect

- Compare RPT/Revenue across 5 years — rising ratio is a warning
- Compare pricing of RPT transactions to disclosed market prices or third-party benchmarks
- Check if the promoter entity receiving business is a "trading arm" with no clear value-add

## Historical Fraud Case Study Patterns (India)

These are general patterns from publicly known cases — for recognition purposes only.

### Pattern A: "Clean for Years, Then Sudden Collapse"

- Company shows pristine governance metrics for years
- Sudden unexplained CFO resignation followed by auditor qualification
- SEBI/SFIO investigation triggered
- **Lesson:** Watch for sudden changes after long clean periods more than persistent small flags

### Pattern B: "Circular Transactions"

- Related party transactions form a circle: Company A sells to Promoter Entity B, which sells back to Company A at a different stage
- Revenue and profit appear inflated
- **Detection:** RPT that is both large and multi-directional (company both buys from and sells to the same promoter entity)

### Pattern C: "Pledge Cascade"

- Promoter pledges 70%+ of shares
- Stock price falls 15% → lender sells pledged shares → price falls more → more margin calls → selling continues
- Company management spending time on stock support rather than business
- **Detection:** High pledging + frequent management commentary on "stock undervaluation"

### Pattern D: "Management Overconfidence → Capital Misallocation"

- Founder CEO with exceptional past success makes large, overpriced acquisition in adjacent field
- Management dismisses concerns in AGM
- 2-3 years later, acquisition written down
- **Detection:** Large acquisition (>30% of market cap) at high multiples + dismissive response to analyst questions about synergies

## How to Read an Annual Report for Governance Signals

### Key Sections to Read

1. **Director's Report** → Remuneration policy, dividend policy, capex plans
2. **Corporate Governance Report** → Board composition, committee memberships, auditor details, related party policy
3. **MD&A (Management Discussion & Analysis)** → Tone, acknowledgment of challenges, forward guidance vs prior year's guidance
4. **Standalone Financial Statements Notes** → Note on Related Party Transactions (typically Note 33-38), Note on KMP Compensation
5. **Auditor's Report** → Emphasis of Matter, Key Audit Matters, any qualifications
6. **Secretarial Audit Report** → Compliance issues flagged

### Green Flags in Annual Reports

- MD&A explicitly references previous year's unfulfilled guidance and explains why
- Auditor's report has no qualifications or emphasis of matter
- RPT section shows declining or stable amounts with clear pricing rationale
- Board has meaningful independent directors (not just family friends)
- Related party policy is published and referenced in governance section

### Red Flags in Annual Reports

- MD&A is boilerplate, same text reused from previous year
- "Emphasis of Matter" in auditor's report (read this very carefully)
- RPT section is buried or difficult to find
- Multiple independent directors resigned in the year
- Secretarial auditor flagged non-compliance items
